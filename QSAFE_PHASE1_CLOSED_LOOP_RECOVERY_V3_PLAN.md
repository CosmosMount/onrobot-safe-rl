# Q_safe Phase 1 v3: closed-loop recovery preregistration

Status: **preregistered design; no v3 simulator outcome has been observed**

Protocol: `config/qsafe_closed_loop_recovery_triage_v3.yaml`

Decision date: 2026-08-09

Objective-2 status: **blocked**

## 1. Decision carried forward from v2

The independent v2 audit falsified the actor-local, one-to-four-step residual
library: every fixed duration reduced audit risk by less than `0.3 pp`, action
ordering stayed at chance, and the discovery-locked best option missed the
`5 pp` practical-effect gate. Those outcomes are permanently consumed and
cannot select or tune this replacement library.

The next experiment therefore changes the intervention, not the Q_safe model.
It asks whether a materially independent, closed-loop recovery behavior can
reduce falls for early SAC policies. No safety model may be trained until this
causal mechanism screen passes.

This ordering follows the support requirement in
[SQRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14603): a safety critic cannot select a safe
action that is absent from its available policy/action support. It also follows
the separation used by [Recovery RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.15920) and the
independent recovery policy in
[Safe Reinforcement Learning for Legged Locomotion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02638),
whose real-robot controller invokes a fixed-duration safety policy. The joint
target recovery candidates are informed by the 12-dimensional joint-target
controller in [ABS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17583), while the one-second
horizon is consistent with the timescale studied in
[Dynamic Fall Recovery](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomimetics9040193). These are
design priors, not evidence for this repository.

## 2. Exact question and evidence boundary

The v3 question is:

> Conditional on a preregistered simulator-only admission rule identifying a
> recoverable-risk state generated by an early 0.30 m/s SAC actor, does one
> discovery-locked closed-loop recovery behavior reduce H96 fall probability
> by at least 5 percentage points, with a one-sided 95% trajectory-cluster
> lower bound of at least 3 points, on independent audit replicas?

The sampled actor ages are `25,438`, `50,030`, and `100,359` environment
steps from one frozen SAC training chain. Two fresh environment/source seeds
are used at every age. This is a bounded mechanism test for the
**SAC-from-scratch route**; it is not yet an online-training result and it does
not establish natural fall incidence or generalization to unseen states. The
admission cohort is deliberately risk-enriched and its estimand is conditional
on screen-positive states. All three checkpoints share SAC training seed 42;
the six source seeds vary simulator trajectories, not SAC training runs.

If the screen passes, it authorizes a new, fresh-data protocol for a state-risk
Q_safe that triggers the locked recovery behavior. The eventual claim, if all
later gates pass, must be phrased as:

> A pretrained Q_safe and pretrained recovery controller reduce cumulative
> falls while the task SAC policy is trained from scratch.

It must not be phrased as though the safety components were also initialized
from scratch. Objective 1 remains incomplete until fresh paired closed-loop
and ten-seed online A/B evidence pass. Speed expansion remains prohibited.

## 4. Locked recovery library

The candidate order, duration, target formula, and tie priority are immutable:

| k | Candidate | Active policy steps | Deployable feedback law |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 0 | nominal early actor | 0 recovery overrides | deterministic early mean at t=0, then stochastic early continuation |
| 1 | mature actor L10 | 10 | deterministic 500k actor on newest 46D frame |
| 2 | mature actor L25 | 25 | same controller for 0.50 s |
| 3 | mature actor L50 | 50 | same controller for 1.00 s |
| 4 | joint brake L10 | 10 | request the newest measured 12D joint position |
| 5 | halfway-neutral L10 | 10 | request `0.5*q_measured + 0.5*q_neutral` |
| 6 | halfway-neutral L25 | 25 | same feedback law for 0.50 s |
| 7 | ramp-neutral L25 | 25 | move prior q target toward neutral by at most 0.04 rad/joint/step |
| 8 | ramp-crouch L25 | 25 | move prior q target toward crouch by at most 0.04 rad/joint/step |

`q_neutral` repeats `[0.05, 0.70, -1.40]` for FR, FL, RR, and RL.
`q_crouch` repeats `[0.05, 0.90, -1.60]`. Joint-space targets are converted by
the production `qpos_to_action` contract and then projected by the unchanged
runtime `ActionApplier` (`kp=60`, `kd=5`, no filter and no max-delta in this
simulation configuration). After an option expires, control returns to the
same early actor. No option is reselected during its active duration.

All behaviors consume only the five corrected deployable 46D frames. The
newest frame has joint position at `0:12` and the prior absolute q target at
`34:46`. The evaluator must reset controller, continuation-policy, disturbance,
and action-projection state for every candidate/replica branch. The exact first
requested/executed/q-target values must be replica-invariant and recorded.

## 5. Source states and admission sampling

Six fresh source seeds are fixed before implementation:

- step 25,438: `7801`, `7802`;
- step 50,030: `7811`, `7812`;
- step 100,359: `7821`, `7822`.

Each seed must contribute exactly 64 accepted groups, for `G=384`. At most one
accepted group may come from a source trajectory. Source trajectories receive
the locked Gaussian impulse program (1.0 m/s linear and 4.0 rad/s angular
standard deviation every ten policy steps). Branch continuations receive no
future exogenous impulse. A state can be proposed only when it is not already a
failure and satisfies the preregistered mechanical pre-screen:
`tilt >= 0.10 rad OR base_height <= 0.32 m`. After a rejected proposal, five
source steps must elapse before the next proposal from that trajectory. Tilt
comes from the deployable quaternion, while base height is privileged and may
be used only for simulator cohort construction; it cannot be an input to a
future Q_safe or its runtime trigger.

Every proposal receives exactly 32 **admission-only nominal** H96 replicas from
a seed domain disjoint from discovery and audit. A proposal is accepted iff
between 6 and 26 replicas, inclusive, fail. This has the same empirical
18.75%--81.25% window as 3--13/16 but sharply reduces low-risk false admission.
The bounds cannot be relaxed, no
top-up may occur, and collection fails if a seed does not reach 64 groups
within 4,096 proposals or 2,048 trajectories. Admission labels never enter
candidate-effect estimates.

The collector writes every proposed identity in order, including rejected
states: source seed, policy age, trajectory/step, state hash, all 32 admission
seed identities, fall/TTF/max-tilt/min-height outcomes, the pre-screen values,
decision reason, protocol hash, and generator commit. Deployable histories and
privileged measurements live in physically separate files.

This admission scheme intentionally changes the target distribution. There is
no inverse-probability or natural-frequency claim: the primary estimand is the
equal-group conditional effect over accepted screen-positive states, stratified
equally by the six source seeds.

## 6. Discovery/audit firewall

For every accepted state, all K=9 candidates receive 64 discovery and 64 audit
common-random-number replicas over H=96 steps. Replica roles use disjoint seed
domains and are assigned before candidate outcomes. Discovery and audit arrays
must be stored in separate files.

Before an audit file can be opened, a no-clobber selection lock must contain:

- protocol, clean generator commit, candidate-library and policy hashes;
- admission-ledger and discovery file/content hashes;
- all group/state identities and the exhaustive seed partition;
- the single global recovery candidate selected on discovery outcomes;
- per-group discovery minimizer tie sets and uniform weights for diagnostics;
- bootstrap specification, exact gates, and a one-shot audit identifier.

The global candidate is the nonnominal candidate with minimum equal-group,
equal-source-seed discovery fall risk. Exact ties are broken by the locked
candidate order above. The audit tool accepts only the exact selection-lock
hash, creates an atomic consumed marker before reading outcomes, and never
tries a runner-up. A failed or interrupted audit remains consumed.

No protected path with a component beginning `formal` or `sealed` may be
listed, opened, hashed, or used. Every v1/v2 result is excluded. Collection
must bind to a clean Git commit containing this protocol and the implementation.

## 7. Preregistered statistics and gates

The statistical unit is one accepted source trajectory/state group (`G=384`),
not any of its 128 replicas. Risk is averaged within group, then equally across
the 64 groups in each source seed, then equally across six seeds. Confidence
bounds use 50,000 hierarchical bootstrap draws: resample source seeds within
each of the three fixed policy-age strata, then resample complete trajectory
groups within each sampled seed. Candidates and replicas are never resampled.
Bootstrap seed is `20260809`.

Because discovery and audit use the same accepted states but independent
branch randomness, audit establishes branch-randomness replication on this
fixed risk-enriched cohort. It is not held-out-state generalization; later
Q_safe, paired, and online protocols must use wholly fresh states and training
seeds.

The data gate requires exactly 384 unique state and trajectory fingerprints,
64 groups per required source seed, K=9, D=64, A=64, H=96, complete candidate
validity, clean hashes, exhaustive disjoint seed roles, and admission counts
6--26. Before any audit file is opened, discovery nominal risk must be between
15% and 75% overall and between 10% and 90% in every policy-age stratum. If it
is not, the cohort is marked inconclusive without opening audit; no more data
are authorized. Audit nominal risk is reported but is not used as a
continue/stop gate for the primary efficacy test.

### Primary global-backup gate

Let `d_global` be audit nominal risk minus audit risk of the single
discovery-locked global candidate. It passes only if all are true:

1. `d_global >= 0.05`;
2. its one-sided 95% hierarchical-cluster lower bound is at least `0.03`;
3. every policy-age stratum has positive audit reduction;
4. all six source-seed reductions are positive.

No pair-order score is required for a globally fixed backup. A pass authorizes
only a fresh protocol for a state-risk Q_safe trigger plus this one locked
backup behavior.

### Conditional state-dependent option gate

This gate is tested only if the primary global gate passes, so it cannot rescue
a failed primary result. Discovery chooses uniform ties among each group's
candidate minima. On independent audit replicas, the state-dependent choice
must satisfy all of:

1. absolute reduction from nominal at least `0.05` with one-sided 95% lower
   bound at least `0.03`;
2. incremental reduction over the locked global candidate at least `0.02`
   with one-sided 95% lower bound greater than zero;
3. discovery-to-audit pair agreement at least `0.58`, with one-sided 95%
   lower bound at least `0.55`;
4. discovery minimizer tie fraction at most 50% and mean winner count at most
   two;
5. every policy-age stratum and all six source seeds have positive
   incremental reduction over the global candidate.

A conditional pass authorizes a fresh option-ranking Q_safe protocol. A
failure leaves only the state-risk trigger plus fixed backup route authorized.

### No-headroom and inconclusive decisions

Audit effects for all eight fixed recovery candidates and the discovery-locked
per-state rule are reported only after the primary decision. A one-sided
simultaneous 95% upper band is constructed over that joint nine-effect vector
while preserving all K9 common-random-number correlations. For bootstrap draw
`b`, let `theta*_bk` be effect `k`; the common critical value is the 0.95
quantile of `max_k(theta_hat_k - theta*_bk)`, and
`U_k = theta_hat_k + critical_value`. Only if the
audit nominal-risk one-sided 95% lower bound is at least `0.10` **and** every
one of those nine effect UCBs is below `0.03` is the library declared to have
no useful headroom. This narrow result covers the K9 fixed behaviors and the
locked discovery rule, not an arbitrary ideal state-dependent oracle. If the
primary fails but the opportunity bound or any effect UCB misses those rules,
the result is **inconclusive**: model training is still forbidden, and neither
audit reuse nor sample top-up is allowed.

## 8. Execution and stop rules

1. Commit this protocol before any v3 simulator outcome.
2. Implement the controller contract, failure-threshold override, admission
   ledger, physical discovery/audit split, selection lock, one-shot auditor,
   schema validation, and deterministic tests.
3. Commit the implementation and require the entire test suite to pass.
4. Collect all six fresh shards once from that clean commit; failed attempts
   are retained in the cohort manifest.
5. Create the discovery selection lock, then consume audit exactly once.
6. Commit the evidence report whether the result passes, fails, or is
   inconclusive.

No Q_safe fitting, paired shield evaluation, online SAC A/B run, or speed-range
expansion is allowed before its preceding gate. If v3 passes, the next
preregistration uses wholly fresh groups and later performs ten paired
from-scratch SAC seeds at a fixed 500k-step exposure with SAC-only,
Q_safe+recovery, and intervention-rate/action-distance-matched random-placebo
arms. Objective 1 still requires at least 20% relative cumulative-fall
reduction, a positive seed-cluster confidence lower bound, at least 95% of SAC
return, and the existing task/deadline gates.
